Achievement Questions Check Your Understanding

1. Which scientist's experiment led to the discovery of the nucleus?

2. Higher-order thinking: If protons all carry the same (positive) charge and like charges repel, why doesn't the nucleus fly apart?

A strong nuclear force between protons and neutrons, acting only at extremely short range inside the nucleus, overcomes the electric repulsion between protons and holds the nucleus together.

3. Why was Thomson's "plum pudding" model rejected after Rutherford's gold foil experiment?

Thomson's model predicted that alpha particles would pass through gold foil with only slight, evenly spread deflection. Instead, most passed straight through (showing the atom is mostly empty space) while a few bounced back sharply (showing a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus) — evidence Thomson's model could not explain.

Homework / Enrichment Going Further

These three topics extend today's lesson — read each short section and answer the question.

1. Millikan's Oil-Drop Experiment (1910)

Robert Millikan sprayed oil droplets between two charged plates and adjusted the electric field strength until a droplet's fall was balanced. By measuring the charge on many droplets, he found they were always a whole-number multiple of one value: 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ coulombs — the charge of a single electron.

Question: Why was it significant that every droplet's charge was a multiple of the same number?

2. The Historical Timeline of the Atomic Model

From 1897 (discovery of the electron) to 1932 (discovery of the neutron) to 2007 (CERN's studies of subatomic particles), our model of the atom has been continually revised as new evidence appeared.

Question: Why is it normal in science for a model (like Thomson's) to be replaced by a better one, rather than a sign that the earlier scientists failed?

3. Seeing Atoms: The Scanning Tunneling Microscope (STM)

An STM image of graphite shows carbon atoms arranged in a repeating hexagonal pattern, each atom appearing as a bright spot a fraction of a nanometer apart from its neighbors.

Question: What do the bright spots in an STM image represent, and why are they evenly spaced?
